Taelor

Product and marketing design for a menswear rental startup: the stylist pages on the website, a gift card flow in the warehouse tool, and what a customer sees when the box arrives.

1. Overview

One designer across website, operations and marketing

Taelor is a menswear rental subscription that pairs human stylists with AI. As the only designer on a small team, I worked on every place a customer or a warehouse worker meets the brand.

WebsiteStylist pages, which I was put in charge of, and the subscriber homepage.
Operations toolA gift card flow, taken from nothing to daily use in the warehouse.
Brand touchpointsPacking sticker, new customer flyer, social posts and Meta ads.

Emotion and trust first. Show how the styling works, not how powerful the AI is.

2. Problem

A growing service whose touchpoints did not match

For customers

  • The stylist page opened with a long biography before any proof.
  • Too many words, and fonts and images that did not fit the brand.
  • The packing sticker and new customer flyer were wordy and off-style.

For the warehouse

  • Physical gift cards had no flow in the operations tool.
  • Two machines could start picking the same order.
  • Missing and in-repair items were tracked on sticky notes.

3. Research

I started by being a customer

  1. Signed up and used the service
  2. Learned the website and the operations tool
  3. Interviewed other interns about onboarding
  4. Wrote up every issue, ranked by risk

What I found, in priority order

PriorityProblemProposed fix
CriticalTwo machines can start the same order, which risks duplicate picking and wrong shipments.Show "In progress" before Start is pressed, and lock the order to one machine.
HighPick lists are not in rack order, so pickers walk back and forth on every order.Sort the list by rack position.
FoundationalRacks are numbered with no logic, so one clothing type is scattered.A letter for the category plus a number, like A01 or B01.
HygieneMissing and in-repair items are tracked on sticky notes.A status tag on the item in the system.
PolishThe packing sticker and new customer flyer are wordy and off-brand.Fewer words, one style, more visuals.

Think from the customer's point of view, even in the wording.

4. Solution

Three places people meet the brand

Website

Stylist pages, redesigned for trust

  • The call to action moved to the top: "Get matched with a stylist".
  • Real customer quotes, with name and date, sit under each look.
  • The biography moved down and got shorter.
  • Previous and next links let visitors browse all ten stylists.

Open any screen to see it larger. Built with the engineer for desktop and phone.

Operations tool

A gift card flow, from zero to in use

  • Mapped how a physical gift card is picked, packed and shipped.
  • The picker types the card number on a phone. The packer types it again on the computer.
  • If the two numbers differ, the tool shows both and highlights the difference.
  • Designed the printed cards and the "How to use your gift card" insert.

The double check

A gift card number is typed twice by two people. A mismatch stops the order before it ships.

Website

A subscriber homepage with clear next steps

Each card is one thing a subscriber can do next: review a collection, make a request for the next shipment, or browse new arrivals.

Unboxing

What a new customer sees in the box

The sticker now says two things in large type. The flyer explains the service in four steps, with a QR code for each action.

Marketing

Ads and social posts

Large type, few words and one clear offer worked best in ads. Social posts moved toward a lifestyle feeling.

What I took away

  • Use the product as a customer before designing for it.
  • Rank problems by risk, so the team knows what to fix first.
  • Fewer words and one clear action, on a page, a sticker or an ad.
