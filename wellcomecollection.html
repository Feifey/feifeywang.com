Wellcome Collection

A mobile exhibition companion that brings exhibition content, the audio guide and spatial audio guidance into one app.

[Prototype link]
Role
Product designer, UI and UX designer, researcher
Timeline
April to May 2025
Team
Solo
Tools

1. Overview

One app for the whole museum visit

Wellcome Collection is a museum in central London built on the belief that everyone's experience of health matters. Its exhibitions aim to include visitors with different needs.

End-to-end experienceInformation architecture through to key flows.
UI and component systemScalable screens and handoff-ready specs.
Prototyped and testedDiscover, navigate, save and revisit.

Accessible is more than compliant content.

2. Problem

Information is split across print, web and borrowed devices

What the museum wanted

  • A more accessible and inclusive visit, with fewer barriers.
  • Clear, on-demand help for different access needs.
  • Accessible ways to experience exhibitions, like audio description.

The friction

  • Exhibition info lives mostly on the web.
  • The audio guide can mean borrowing a device or hunting for a page.
  • This hits blind and low-vision visitors hardest, right when they are moving.

3. Research

What "accessible" means to a visitor

I audited the museum's physical and digital touchpoints through field observation and competitive scanning.

IndependentPreview, navigate and revisit content in one place.
Low-friction navigationLess stopping, asking for help or switching tools.
Understanding in real timeThe right information in context: where I am, what is nearby, what to do next.

How I tested and refined it

  1. 01
    Wireframes and sketchesTested guided discovery on mobile, with an optional spatial audio tour.
  2. 02
    Audit, observation and feedbackWeb-first content and a separate audio guide caused friction, so I moved to one app.
  3. 03

Guidance breaks down when it lives outside the main experience.

4. Solution

Content, audio guide and wayfinding in one flow

Gallery discovery

What's on, exhibition summaries and locations in a native, scannable list.

Demo: scrolling the Galleries list of current and upcoming exhibitions.

Audio guide inside the visit

Start the audio guide straight from an exhibition page. No borrowed device.

Demo: opening the Being Human exhibition page and its audio guide.

Keypad lookup

Type a room or object number to jump to it, without digging through menus.

Multi-level wayfinding

A floor plan for every level shows where galleries and amenities are. A visitor hub keeps opening times, step-free routes and contact links in one place.

Concept extension: spatial audio wayfinding

When tactile cues are not consistent across floors, an optional spatial audio mode helps blind and low-vision visitors move between stops. The sound shifts as the visitor turns.

5. Reflection

A tested structure, ready for the gallery

One end-to-end journeyBefore, during and after the visit in one system.
Less "search and borrow"Audio guide and content move into one mobile flow.
Self-directed wayfindingStart or stop guidance without staff or extra hardware.

Next for the app

  • Pilot usability tests in the gallery.
  • Check every accessibility mode end to end: screen reader, text scaling, reduced motion, high contrast.
  • Prototype spatial audio with NFC or QR start points.
  • Connect content to before, during and after the visit.

What I took away

  • When accessibility leads the structure, the experience is clearer for everyone.
  • Designing for in-the-moment use means clarity and timing come before feature count.
  • Prototyping is a tool for thinking and alignment, not only validation.
